Five green scenarios

An eval is a measurement, and every measurement has error bars. Today you learn how wide yours are, how to tell a real regression from noise, and which grader to use for each check in D'Garage.

PREDICT FIRST

What do 5 of 5 passing scenarios prove?

D'Garage has five golden scenarios in FLUJO_PILOTO §16. Suppose all five pass. With 95% confidence, what is the lowest pass rate your qualifier could really have?

At least 57%. Five passes are fully compatible with a qualifier that fails 4 in 10 real conversations. Your five scenarios prove each path works once. They are acceptance tests. They say little about a failure rate. The lab below shows why.
01 · THE MENTAL MODEL

Evals are a loop that ends in a decision

Every eval, from a notebook script to a CI gate, has the same five steps. Most teams stop at step 3 and read the average. The two steps that matter most are the last two.

CasesGolden, adversarial and real-traffic inputs
→
RunSame prompt version on every case, outputs stored
→
ScoreAssertions, LLM judge or human per check
→
CompareNew vs baseline, with an interval on the difference
→
DecideSHIP, BLOCK, or INCONCLUSIVE
02 · LAB A

How wide are your error bars?

A pass rate measured on n cases is only an estimate. The Wilson interval gives the range the true rate could be in, and unlike the textbook formula it stays honest at 0% and 100%. Move the sliders.

Wilson interval explorer

95% confidence

Rule of thumb: the interval width shrinks with √n. Four times the cases halves the error bar. To detect a 5-point drop you need around 200 cases. To compare two close prompts, you need 500 or more.

03 · WHO GRADES WHAT

04 · RUBRICS

A judge is only as good as its anchors

"Rate 1 to 5" leaves the judge to invent what a 3 means, and it invents something different on every call. Anchoring each level to observable behaviour cuts judge variance by roughly a third. Compare:

Unanchored

Rate from 1 to 5 how good the tone of this WhatsApp reply is.

Two runs on the same reply return 3 and 4. You can't tell if the new prompt helped.

Anchored
  1. Uses usted/tú consistently, greets by name, one clear next step, under 60 words, no emojis unless the lead used them.
  2. As 5 but slightly long, or the next step is implied instead of stated.
  3. Correct but generic: no name, or no next step.
  4. Stiff or pushy, mixes usted and tú, or uses Latin American phrasing.
  5. Rude, invents facts, or pressures the lead.

Comparing two prompt versions? Use a pairwise judge ("which reply is better, A or B?") and run it twice with the order swapped. It avoids the question of what a "4" means, and swapping the order cancels the judge's bias toward whichever answer it sees first.

05 · LAB B

The regression gate

You changed the qualifier prompt. The old version passed 94% and the new one passes 88%. Is it worse, or is that noise? Don't compare the two averages. Build an interval on the difference (Newcombe's method, built from the two Wilson intervals), then apply three rules:

  • BLOCK the whole interval is below 0, so the new version is confidently worse.
  • SHIP the interval's lower end is above −margin, so any drop is smaller than you accept.
  • INCONCLUSIVE anything else. You don't know yet, so add cases.

  1. wilson_ci(successes, total): the interval from Lab A.
  2. diff_ci(...): Newcombe's interval for the difference.
  3. gate(...): the three rules from Lab B, with BLOCK checked first.
  4. check_qualifier(expected, actual): the deterministic D'Garage assertions from section 03.

The formulas, with p = s/n and z = 1.96:

Wilson
  denom  = 1 + z²/n
  center = (p + z²/(2n)) / denom
  half   = z · √( p(1−p)/n + z²/(4n²) ) / denom
  → (max(0, center − half), min(1, center + half))

Newcombe, for d = p_new − p_base
  (l_b, u_b) = wilson(base)     (l_n, u_n) = wilson(new)
  lower = d − √( (p_new − l_n)² + (u_b − p_base)² )
  upper = d + √( (u_n − p_new)² + (p_base − l_b)² )

Then compare with the repo's version in phases/11-llm-engineering/10-evaluation/code/eval_framework.py. Its compare_eval_runs flags a regression when the mean drops by more than 0.3, and ignores the intervals it just computed. Spot why that's a weaker gate than yours.
